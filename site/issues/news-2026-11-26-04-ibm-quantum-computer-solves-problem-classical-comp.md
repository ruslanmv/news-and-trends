## What Happened

IBM and University of Chicago researchers have achieved a groundbreaking milestone in the field of quantum computing by solving a complex problem that classical computers cannot handle in just 15 minutes. This breakthrough opens up new possibilities for research and development in the field of artificial intelligence and machine learning.

The problem in question was the optimization of a critical chemical reaction in the pharmaceutical industry. Classical computers would take days or even weeks to reach a solution, but the quantum computer produced the answer in a matter of minutes. This significant speedup is due to the quantum computer's ability to perform parallel calculations and overcome the limitations of classical computers' sequential processing.

The researchers employed a novel error-detection technique, which allowed them to establish confidence in the accuracy of the results, which is a major hurdle in quantum computing due to the inherent measurement errors in quantum experiments.

## Why It Matters

This groundbreaking achievement has a wide range of implications for the pharmaceutical industry, drug discovery, and materials science. The ability to solve complex problems in a fraction of the time will allow researchers and scientists to make significant advancements in these fields.

The discovery also has the potential to revolutionize the development of new drugs and therapies, leading to faster and more efficient cures for diseases.

## Context & Background

The field of quantum computing is still in its early stages of development, but it holds immense potential for the future. Quantum computers have the ability to perform calculations that are impossible for classical computers, such as simulating molecular interactions and optimizing complex systems.

The researchers' work on this problem was funded by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), and it is hoped that this achievement will lead to the development of commercial quantum computers that can be used for a variety of applications, including drug discovery, materials science, and financial modeling.

## What to Watch Next

The researchers are currently working on developing a quantum computer that can perform even more complex calculations. They also plan to explore the use of quantum computing for other challenges in the pharmaceutical industry, such as predicting drug efficacy and optimizing drug delivery.
