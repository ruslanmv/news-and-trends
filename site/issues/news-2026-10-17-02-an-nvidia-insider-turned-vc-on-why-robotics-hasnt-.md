## What Happened

Amulya Vishwanath, the former Head of Product Marketing at NVIDIA, has shared his perspective on the ongoing development of robotics. While robots have made significant strides in recent years, with successful applications like ChatGPT and self-driving cars, Vishwanath believes that robotics is currently in its "Lego era," with the basic building blocks like actuators, robotic hands, software, and safety standards still being rebuilt.

He argues that despite these advancements, the core components of robotics are still being developed and are not yet fully integrated into the field. "It's like building a house with Lego bricks," he said during an interview. "We're piecing together the pieces, but the foundation is still being built."

This view runs counter to the popular belief that robotics is poised to revolutionize various industries, including manufacturing, healthcare, and customer service. However, Vishwanath's perspective highlights the ongoing challenges and limitations in building a fully functional and widely accepted robot workforce.

## Why It Matters

The main significance of Vishwanath's perspective is that it underscores the need for continued investment and collaboration among industry players, government agencies, and research institutions. He argues that until the foundation of robotics is built, despite the impressive advancements, the industry will not reach its full potential.

Additionally, it suggests that companies should focus on building a more robust and standardized ecosystem, including collaboration with academia and fostering a culture of shared innovation. By addressing these challenges, industry players can work together to create a sustainable ecosystem for continued innovation in the field.

## Context & Background

The news article highlights the slow development of robotics due to the ongoing challenges in building a broad range of robots with diverse functions. It also emphasizes the need for a collaborative effort between industry players, government and academia to address these challenges and accelerate the adoption of robots across multiple industries.
