## What Happened

The New York State Department of Consumer Affairs (NYDCA) has announced a new set of regulations that will require major AI developers like OpenAI and Anthropic to register, disclose safety practices, and report critical incidents or face fines. These new regulations are intended to ensure that these companies are operating their AI systems responsibly and transparently.

**Registration:** 

According to the announcement, companies must register their AI systems with the NYDCA within 90 days of the law's implementation. This registration process will include providing information about the AI system, including its purpose, capabilities, and data sources. 

**Transparency:** 

Companies must also disclose their safety practices and risk assessments related to their AI systems to the NYDCA. This transparency requirement will help consumers and regulators understand how these companies are managing the potential risks associated with their AI products.

**Critical Incident Reporting:** 

Companies must also report any critical incidents related to their AI systems to the NYDCA within 24 hours of the incident. Critical incidents can include security breaches, data leaks, or instances of AI-powered products causing harm to users.

## Why It Matters

The new AI safety law is a significant step forward in protecting consumers and ensuring the responsible development and use of AI technology in New York State. These regulations are designed to ensure that these companies are operating their AI systems responsibly and transparently. This will help to mitigate the potential risks associated with AI, such as privacy violations and biases.

## Context & Background

The AI safety law is a response to the rapid growth of AI technology. AI technology is already being used in a wide range of applications, from facial recognition and self-driving cars to medical diagnosis and customer service. However, this growth has also raised concerns about the potential risks associated with AI. These concerns include privacy violations, biases, and the potential for autonomous weapons.

The NYDCA has a long history of regulating the financial and healthcare industries. The new AI safety law is modeled on these existing regulations, but it is the first law in New York State specifically addressing AI safety.

## What to Watch Next

The NYDCA will begin accepting registrations for the AI safety registration program starting in January 2027. The NYDCA will also begin accepting submissions for critical incident reports starting in February 2027. The NYDCA plans to use this information to enforce the new law and to conduct ongoing oversight of the AI industry.
